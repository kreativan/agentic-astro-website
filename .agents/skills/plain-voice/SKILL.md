# Plain voice

Write like a capable person explaining their work to a client over coffee.
Short sentences, ordinary words, concrete things. The reader should understand
every line on the first read and never feel sold to.

## Rules

1. **Say what it is and what it does.** Name the thing, then the result for
   the reader. "Custom CRM and ERP for small businesses." Not "Empowering
   businesses with next-generation solutions."
2. **Short sentences.** Mostly under 15 words. One idea per sentence. A fragment
   is fine when it lands: "It's boring on purpose."
3. **Plain words.** Use the word a client would use: "build", "run", "fix",
   "keep it running", "website", "app", "your team". If a simpler word works,
   use it.
4. **Concrete over abstract.** Name real things: clients, invoices, offers,
   backups, the order in your inbox. List them instead of summarising them
   ("backups, updates, monitoring and fixes").
5. **First person, talking to "you".** "I build" or "we build" (whichever the
   site uses, consistently), "you don't have to think about it". Write as the
   people doing the work, to the person reading.
6. **Honest and specific.** Say what is true today: "coming soon", "for
   clients only", "I don't rent out servers". Never invent clients, numbers,
   results or testimonials. No claims you can't back up.
7. **Calm confidence.** No hype, no exclamation marks, no urgency. Let the
   facts do the work. A dry line is welcome: "It's part of how I build, not an
   add-on."
8. **Contrast to explain.** "Not X. Y." is the main rhetorical tool, used
   sparingly: "Not just a configurator. A complete webshop."
9. **Headlines are plain statements** that end with a full stop: "What clients
   say.", "Pick a module. Shape it to your business." No questions-as-hooks, no
   puns that need decoding.
10. **Technical terms only when the reader needs them**, and explained in the
    same breath: "The backend is never on the front line. The sites only meet
    it at build time."
11. **Light punctuation.** Full stops and commas. A colon to introduce a list.
    No em dashes as decoration, no semicolon chains, no ellipses.
12. **Cut, then cut again.** Remove every word that doesn't change the meaning:
    "really", "very", "actually", "in order to", "it's important to note".

## Words to avoid

These read as hype or as AI-written. Replace them with the plain thing they
stand for.

| Avoid | Write instead |
|-------|---------------|
| leverage, utilize | use |
| seamless, seamlessly | (say what actually works: "in one place", "without re-typing") |
| robust, powerful, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-generation | (name the actual quality: fast, tested, secure, kept up to date) |
| empower, unlock, unleash, supercharge, elevate, transform | help, let you, make |
| solution(s) as filler | the actual thing: website, app, CRM, shop |
| delve, dive into, explore | look at, see |
| journey, landscape, realm, ecosystem, tapestry | (drop it) |
| harness, streamline (as filler), optimize (as filler) | (say what changes: "takes minutes", "done for you") |
| game-changer, revolutionary, innovative, world-class | (drop it, show the fact) |
| In today's fast-paced digital world… | (start with the point) |
| Whether you're X or Y… | (say who it's for directly) |
| Not only… but also… | two short sentences |
| "It's not just X, it's Y" more than once per page | use the contrast once, where it matters |
| Rule-of-three adjective stacks ("fast, secure and scalable") everywhere | one concrete fact |

Also avoid: emojis in copy, exclamation marks, rhetorical questions as
openers, "Let's" openers, ending sections with a moral ("Because your business
deserves…").

## Before and after

> Before: Unlock the full potential of your business with our cutting-edge,
> AI-powered CRM solution that seamlessly streamlines your workflows.
>
> After: A CRM built around how your team works. Clients, quotes, invoices and
> reports in one place.

> Before: We provide robust, end-to-end managed hosting services designed to
> empower your digital presence.
>
> After: I host and run the projects I build. Backups, updates, monitoring and
> fixes, so you don't have to think about any of it.

> Before: Are you ready to take your website to the next level?
>
> After: Tell me what you need and I'll come back with next steps.

## Examples of the voice (from kreativan.dev, built on this starter)

- "Every project starts with your business, not a template. I plan it, build
  it, connect it and keep it running."
- "I don't rent out servers. I run the projects I build, so the person keeping
  it online is the person who knows how it works."
- "Everyone works in the same backend, but nobody sees more than they should."
- "Updates, fixes and new features go through one pipeline. It's boring on
  purpose."
- "People still visit. But more and more, an AI reads your site for them, or
  an agent does the job for them. I build for all three."

## Formats

- **Headline:** 2–7 words, a plain statement, full stop.
- **Lede:** 1–3 short sentences. What it is, who it's for, what changes.
- **Button:** verb + object, 2–4 words: "Start a project", "See it in action",
  "Email me". An arrow (→) is fine for links that move you on.
- **Card / list item:** a bold name and one line, under 12 words.
- **Meta description:** one or two sentences, about 150 characters, saying
  what the page is and who it's for. No keyword stuffing.
- **Kicker / eyebrow:** plain lowercase words: "kind words", "how it works".
- **Email:** greeting, the point in the first line, what happens next, sign
  off. No "I hope this email finds you well".

## Checklist before handing text over

- [ ] Could a non-technical client read it once and explain it back?
- [ ] No word from the avoid list, no hype, no exclamation marks
- [ ] Every claim is true and specific; nothing invented
- [ ] Sentences mostly under 15 words; no filler words left
- [ ] Headlines are statements with a full stop
- [ ] It sounds like one person talking, not a brochure
